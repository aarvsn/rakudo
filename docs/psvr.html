Peripheral line

PlayStation VR & VR2

CUH-ZVR1/2 · CFI-ZVR1

PS VR launched in October 2016 as an external headset tethered to the PS4 via a breakout processing box; PS VR2 launched in February 2023 as a PS5-exclusive successor with a direct single-cable USB-C connection and no external processing unit, since the PS5's own hardware absorbed the reprojection/compositing work the original PS VR box performed externally.

§7.1Core specifications

PS VR display5.7" OLED, 1920×1080 total (960×1080 per eye), 90/120 Hz, ~100° field of view.
PS VR processingExternal "Processor Unit" box handles social screen mirroring, 3D audio, and a technique called reprojection to smooth frame delivery; the PS4 itself renders the game.
PS VR2 displayDual 2000×2040 OLED panels (one per eye), 90/120 Hz, ~110° field of view, HDR support, eye-tracking built in (enabling foveated rendering).
PS VR2 connectionSingle USB-C cable directly to the PS5, no external breakout box; the PS5's own SoC performs all compositing.
TrackingPS VR: external PlayStation Camera, outside-in tracking. PS VR2: inside-out tracking via four embedded cameras on the headset itself, no external camera required.

Emulation status: Neither PS VR nor PS VR2 has a meaningful standalone software emulator, since VR titles depend on the host console's GPU output and real-time head tracking rather than the headset containing independent "hardware" to emulate in the traditional sense. PS VR (1) titles are, in practice, addressed by whatever level of PS4 emulation shadPS4 reaches, played back on a PC VR headset via community-built compatibility shims rather than a dedicated PS VR emulator.
