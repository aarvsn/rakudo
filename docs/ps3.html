Generation 3 · Codename "Cell Broadband Engine"

PlayStation 3

CECHA00 → CECH-4300

Launched in Japan on 11 November 2006, the PS3 paired a radically unconventional CPU — the Cell Broadband Engine, co-developed with IBM and Toshiba — with an Nvidia GPU derived from the GeForce 7 architecture. Its asymmetric multi-core design (one general-purpose core plus eight specialised co-processors) made it exceptionally powerful for well-suited workloads and exceptionally hard to program for and, later, to emulate.

§4.1Core architecture

CPU"Cell Broadband Engine" — one dual-threaded PowerPC-based PPE (Power Processing Element) at 3.2 GHz, plus 8 SPEs (Synergistic Processing Elements, SIMD-only cores with 256 KB local store each); one SPE is disabled for yield/redundancy and one is reserved by the OS hypervisor, leaving 6 SPEs available to game developers.
GPU"RSX Reality Synthesizer" — Nvidia G70/G71-derived (GeForce 7800 GTX class), 550 MHz, unified-ish but still fixed-function-leaning pipeline predating DirectX 10-class unified shaders; famously RAM-starved relative to the CPU's needs.
Main RAM (system)256 MB XDR DRAM (Rambus), accessed by the Cell PPE/SPEs.
Video RAM256 MB GDDR3, dedicated to RSX; the two memory pools are not unified, requiring careful manual data placement by developers — a further source of emulation complexity.
Optical driveBlu-ray Disc (2x, later 6x on Slim/Super Slim), backward-reads DVD and CD.
PS1/PS2 backward compatibilityOriginal 60 GB/20 GB launch units (CECHA/CECHB, "Phat") included real Emotion Engine + Graphics Synthesizer silicon for full hardware BC. Later 2007 revisions moved to software-emulated PS2 BC with reduced compatibility; from late 2007 onward PS2 BC was dropped entirely except in select PAL markets, while PS1 BC (software-emulated) was retained across the whole product line.
OutputHDMI 1.3 (first PlayStation with digital video out), component, composite; native output up to 1080p.
Storage2.5" SATA HDD, user-swappable on every revision, from 20 GB at launch up to 500 GB on late Super Slim units.
Process node90 nm Cell/RSX at launch, shrunk through 65 nm, 45 nm, and finally a combined 40 nm Cell+RSX die on Super Slim units for drastically reduced power draw and the elimination of the notorious "Yellow Light of Death" hardware failures common on Phat/Slim units.

§4.2Notable model variants

CECHA00 / CECHB00 "Phat" (2006)

Launch, 60 GB/20 GB

Full hardware PS2 backward compatibility, 4 USB ports, memory card reader, Wi-Fi (60 GB SKU only).

CECHExx–CECHHxx (2007–08)

Cost-reduced Phat

PS2 hardware BC removed; software emulation only, then removed outright on most SKUs.

CECH-2xxx "Slim" (2009)

Major redesign

~66% smaller, 45 nm Cell, glossy top, no PS2 BC, introduced the "PS3 Slim" curved form factor.

CECH-4xxx "Super Slim" (2012)

Final revision

Sliding disc cover instead of a slot/tray, further shrunk 40 nm combined die, up to 500 GB HDD.

§4.3Emulation guide

RECOMMENDED
RPCS3 — the only actively maintained PS3 emulator, in development since 2011 with dramatic accuracy gains from 2017 onward.
ACCURACY
HIGH — the majority of the retail library is playable at full speed on strong host hardware; PPU (PowerPC) emulation is essentially solved, SPU emulation (recompiled JIT) is the main remaining performance-sensitive area.
FIRMWARE REQUIRED
Yes. RPCS3 requires the official PS3 system firmware (a PS3UPDAT.PUP file, obtainable from Sony's own update servers) to be installed inside the emulator to supply system libraries (sprx modules) that RPCS3 does not reimplement from scratch — this is a firmware install, not a proprietary BIOS dump, and does not require owning a PS3 to obtain legally.
HOST REQUIREMENT
Demanding by emulation standards: a modern 6+ core / 12+ thread CPU with high per-core performance is strongly recommended for SPU-heavy titles, plus a Vulkan-capable GPU roughly equivalent to a GTX 1660 / RX 5600 or better for full-speed, native-resolution play; some of the most demanding first-party titles remain difficult to sustain at a locked framerate even on high-end 2025-class hardware.
KNOWN ISSUES
A minority of titles that lean heavily on precise SPU scheduling or PS3-specific GPU quirks (RSX "flip" timing) can exhibit stutter, audio crackle, or minor visual differences; PS2/PS1 backward-compatible discs played on a PS3 are not covered by RPCS3 and should instead be run on PCSX2/DuckStation directly.
