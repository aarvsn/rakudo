Legal notes

This is general information, not legal advice, and copyright law varies by country.

  • Emulator software itself is broadly legal to write, distribute, and use — this has been tested and upheld in court in multiple jurisdictions (notably Sony v. Connectix and Sony v. Bleem! in the United States, both concerning PS1 emulation).
  • Dumping a BIOS, firmware image, or game ROM from hardware and media you personally own, for your own personal backup/use, is generally treated as fair use or a comparable personal-use exception in many jurisdictions — but downloading someone else's dump of the same file, even for a console you also own, is typically still infringement, because the "personal use" reasoning applies to your own act of copying, not to redistribution.
  • Circumventing DRM/copy protection to make that personal backup can itself raise separate legal issues in some jurisdictions (e.g. anti-circumvention provisions under the US DMCA), independent of the copyright status of the underlying data.
  • Selling, sharing, or hosting ROM/ISO/BIOS files for others is copyright infringement essentially everywhere, regardless of whether the original hardware is out of production.
