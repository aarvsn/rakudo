Handheld line · Codename "Kunoichi"

PlayStation Portable

PSP-1000 → PSP-N1000 (Go)

Launched in Japan on 12 December 2004, the PSP was Sony's first handheld and the first portable to natively play movies and games from a proprietary optical disc format (UMD). It shares architectural DNA with the PS2 — its main CPU is a MIPS core descended from the same lineage as the Emotion Engine, running a custom real-time OS rather than a cut-down PS2 kernel.

§3.1Core architecture

CPUCustom Sony "Allegrex" — MIPS R4000-derived 32-bit core, clocked at 1–333 MHz dynamically, officially capped at 222 MHz for retail firmware (raised to 333 MHz via the "CPU speed" hack widely used by homebrew and later officially permitted for some titles).
Media EngineA second, largely identical Allegrex core dedicated to audio/video decode (UMD movie playback, ATRAC3 audio), plus a VME (vector/media coprocessor) shared conceptually with the PS2's VU design philosophy.
GPUCustom Sony GE (Graphics Engine), fixed-function T&L pipeline, no unified shaders; comparable in throughput to a mid-range PS2 GS for 2D/3D workloads at handheld power budgets.
Main RAM32 MB (PSP-1000) → 64 MB (PSP-2000 onward) embedded DRAM.
Display4.3" TFT LCD, 480×272 native resolution, 16:9 aspect ratio — unusually wide for its era.
StorageUMD (Universal Media Disc, 1.8 GB single-layer optical) for retail games/movies; Memory Stick PRO Duo for saves, and from the PSP Go onward, 16 GB internal flash with UMD dropped entirely in favour of the PlayStation Store.
Wireless802.11b Wi-Fi (ad-hoc and infrastructure), IrDA on early units; Bluetooth added only on the PSP Go.
BatteryRemovable lithium-ion, 1200–2200 mAh depending on revision.

§3.2Notable model variants

PSP-1000 "Fat" (2004)

Launch model

32 MB RAM, no built-in microphone, most susceptible to analog stick drift of any revision.

PSP-2000 "Slim & Lite" (2007)

Redesign

64 MB RAM (unlocks higher-fidelity later titles), ~33% lighter, adds TV-out via component cable, built-in mic.

PSP-3000 (2008)

Screen revision

Anti-glare LCD with drastically reduced input lag / ghosting, second built-in microphone.

PSP-N1000 "Go" (2009)

Digital-only, slide form factor

No UMD drive, 16 GB internal storage, Bluetooth, smaller 3.8" screen — commercially unsuccessful due to losing compatibility with existing UMD libraries.

PSP-E1000 "Street" (2011)

Budget SKU

Wi-Fi removed, mono speaker only, sold at reduced price in PAL regions.

§3.3Emulation guide

RECOMMENDED
PPSSPP — the de facto standard, actively developed since 2012, available natively on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS.
ACCURACY
FULL — compatibility is effectively complete for the retail library; PPSSPP is widely regarded as one of the most accurate console emulators ever written, in part because it also functions as a from-scratch reimplementation of the PSP's system libraries rather than a pure low-level hardware emulator.
BIOS REQUIRED
No. PPSSPP HLE-implements the PSP's firmware/kernel calls directly, so no BIOS dump is needed for the large majority of titles. A small number of titles benefit from optional font/plugin files for correct text rendering.
HOST REQUIREMENT
Extremely low — PPSSPP runs at full native speed on essentially any smartphone from the last eight years; upscaled rendering (2x–8x internal resolution) is achievable on any 2015-or-later discrete or integrated GPU.
KNOWN ISSUES
A handful of titles depend on precise Media Engine audio-decode timing for cutscene sync; some homebrew relying on undocumented syscalls is incompatible by design.
